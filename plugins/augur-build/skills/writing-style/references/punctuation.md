# Punctuation

Detail for the em-dash rule in the `writing-style` skill.

## Replacing the em-dash

The em-dash (`—`) is a strong sign of machine-written text: language models use far more of them than people do, and readers notice. Almost every one has a plainer replacement:

| The em-dash is doing | Use |
|----------------------|-----|
| A break for emphasis | A period: start a new sentence |
| A parenthetical aside | Commas, or parentheses for a true aside |
| A range (5—10) | An en-dash (`–`) or "to" |
| Introducing a list or an example | A colon |
| Joining two related clauses | A period or a semicolon |

```
❌ The wiki is the spec — the repo is the implementation.
✅ The wiki is the spec. The repo is the implementation.

❌ Three audiences read the wiki — junior devs, senior devs, and AI agents.
✅ Three audiences read the wiki: junior devs, senior devs, and AI agents.
```

## When an em-dash stays

- In a quotation that has one: keep the original.
- An aside where commas would be ambiguous and parentheses too formal. Even then, rewording is usually better.

New text defaults to periods, commas and colons. When editing old text, replace em-dashes along the way. Reaching for one is a sign the sentence wants to be two.
